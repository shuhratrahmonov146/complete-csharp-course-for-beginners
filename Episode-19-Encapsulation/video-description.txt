What if one line of code could set your bank balance to -500? With a public setter, it can. In this lesson, you learn encapsulation in C#: keep an object's data private, and change it only through public methods that check the rules. We build a real BankAccount class in VS Code with a private decimal balance, a read-only Balance property, and Deposit and Withdraw methods that reject invalid amounts without ever corrupting the balance.

This is episode 19 of the Complete C# Course for Beginners on ITHubCoding. It continues from episode 18, C# Constructors: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OkaUMwhafMQ
The full course is in the playlist.

WHAT YOU WILL LEARN
• What encapsulation means: data and behavior together, with controlled access
• public vs private, and why Program.cs cannot touch a private field (real error CS0122)
• Why a public set is dangerous for data with rules
• A private field plus a read-only property (get only), and what happens when you try to assign it (real error CS0200)
• A Deposit method that rejects zero and negative amounts
• A Withdraw method that also rejects amounts larger than the balance
• get, set, and private set: who can read and who can write
• When a public get and set is perfectly fine
• Common mistakes, and why decimal is the right type for money

THE PROGRAM
EncapsulationBasics deposits 100, -20 and 0, then withdraws 30, 500 and -5. The real output: "Deposited 100. Balance: 100", "Deposit rejected: -20", "Deposit rejected: 0", "Withdrew 30. Balance: 70", "Not enough money for 500.", "Withdrawal rejected: -5", and "Final balance: 70". Every rejected request leaves the balance exactly as it was.

YOUR CHALLENGE
Create a Temperature class with a private decimal celsius field. Rule: a temperature can never be below -273.15 °C (absolute zero). Add a read-only Celsius property and a SetCelsius method that rejects invalid values. Test 25, -300, and -273.15. Details are in the pinned comment.
