What kind of value can a C# variable hold? In this lesson, we extend the Student Profile App from Episode 5 into a student report and learn the five data types every beginner needs: string, int, double, decimal, and bool. Everything is a real recording in VS Code, with real builds, real output, and real compiler errors.

This is episode 6 of the Complete C# Course for Beginners on ITHubCoding. It continues from Episode 5 (C# Variables Explained). Earlier episodes are in the course playlist.

WHAT YOU WILL LEARN
• Why data types matter: "22" + 1 prints 221, but 22 + 1 prints 23
• string: text in double quotes, and why a word without quotes is an error (CS0103)
• int: whole numbers, their range, and what happens when you put text into an int (CS0029)
• double: numbers with decimals, stored approximately (0.1 + 0.2 = 0.30000000000000004)
• decimal: exact values for money, and the m suffix (249.99m)
• bool: true or false, and why the output shows True with a capital T
• How to choose a type for what a value means, not for how it looks
• Common mistakes with real compiler errors: CS0029, CS0664, CS0266
• A complete student report: values typed by the user, set by the program, and calculated (passed = a score of at least 60)

THE APP
StudentProfileApp asks for your name, age, and course, and prints a student report with an average score (double), a course fee (decimal), and a passed result (bool) that the program calculates from the score. Invalid input such as "twenty" for the age doesn't crash it.
