Programs need to make decisions. In this lesson, you learn if, else, and else if in C#, step by step, and then build a real Grade Checker in VS Code. It reads a score, checks it with int.TryParse, rejects impossible scores, and prints a grade and a pass or fail result. Everything is a real recording: real code, real output, a real compiler error, and real bugs found by testing.

This is episode 9 of the Complete C# Course for Beginners on ITHubCoding. It continues from Episode 8 (Operators and the calculator). Earlier episodes are in the course playlist.

WHAT YOU WILL LEARN
• What an if statement is: a condition in parentheses, and a block in braces
• Why every comparison gives a bool: true or false
• The six comparison operators: equal, not equal, greater than, less than, and the two "or equal" versions
• else: exactly one of two blocks runs
• else if: a chain checked from top to bottom, where the first true condition wins
• Why the order of the conditions matters
• Safe input with int.TryParse, and a range check with || (or)
• Common mistakes: = instead of == (error CS0029), a forgotten boundary, the wrong order, a semicolon after the condition (warning CS0642), and no range check
• Boundary testing: 100, 90, 89, 80, 60, 59, 0, -1, 101, and text

THE APP
GradeChecker asks for a score from 0 to 100. Text and decimal numbers are rejected by int.TryParse with a helpful message, and scores below 0 or above 100 are rejected by the range check. An else if chain picks the grade: 90 to 100 is an A, 80 to 89 a B, 70 to 79 a C, 60 to 69 a D, and below 60 an F. A second decision prints Pass (60 or more) or Fail.
These are example grading rules for this lesson. Real schools and countries often use different ones, so change the numbers to match yours.
