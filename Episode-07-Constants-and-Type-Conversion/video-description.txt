How do you keep a value from ever changing, and how do you turn text from the keyboard into a number safely? In this lesson, we build a real Course Fee Calculator in VS Code and learn C# constants and type conversion step by step. Everything is a real recording, with real builds, real output, real compiler errors, and two real bugs found by testing.

This is episode 7 of the Complete C# Course for Beginners on ITHubCoding. It continues from Episode 6 (C# Data Types Explained). Earlier episodes are in the course playlist.

WHAT YOU WILL LEARN
• Constants: const decimal DiscountRate = 0.10m; and why reassigning it is error CS0131
• Why keyboard input is always text (CS0029: string to decimal)
• decimal.TryParse: convert text safely, with a helpful message instead of a crash
• Implicit conversion (int to decimal) vs. explicit conversion with a cast: (int)149.99m is 149
• Calculating a discount and a final fee with decimal
• Formatting money with F2 and percentages with P0, without assuming a currency
• Common mistakes: the missing m suffix (CS0664) and decimal.Parse crashing on text (FormatException)
• Testing with expected results first, and fixing two real bugs: a negative fee, and a receipt that was off by one cent

THE APP
CourseFeeCalculator asks for the original course fee, rejects text and negative values with a helpful message, applies a constant 10% discount, rounds the discount to whole cents, and prints the original fee, discount rate, discount amount, and final fee. Example: 200.00 gives a discount of 20.00 and a final fee of 180.00.
